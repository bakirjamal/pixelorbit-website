ResumeFrom a blank page to a job-ready resume.

A career-tech company needed a resume builder that job seekers could use without design skills or a writing coach. We designed and built it in-house.

01 — The problem

Good people,bad resumes.

Most job seekers write their resume in a word processor, from scratch, the night before they apply. The resume ends up hurting them more than helping.

01

The blank page

People know their work but don't know what to write, how much to write, or which sections a resume needs.

02

Layouts that break

Add one line in a word processor and the whole layout shifts onto a second page.

03

Rejected by software

Fancy designs with tables and graphics often can't be read by the applicant tracking systems many companies use to screen CVs.

04

One resume for every job

Tailoring a resume for each role means copying files and editing by hand, so most people send the same one everywhere.

02 — The approach

Write on the left,see it on the right.

We built the whole product around one idea: users fill in their story section by section, and a live, print-ready page updates next to them. Content and design stay separate, so the layout never breaks.

01

03

04
